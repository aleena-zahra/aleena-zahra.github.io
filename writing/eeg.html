What is EEG?

September 2026 · 8 min read

Electroencephalography, or EEG, is one of the most widely used tools for measuring electrical activity in the human brain. But what is it actually measuring?

What does EEG measure?

EEG records tiny voltage differences from electrodes placed on the scalp. These signals reflect the combined electrical activity of large populations of neurons.

Why does the signal look so noisy?

EEG is incredibly sensitive. Along with neural activity, the electrodes can capture eye movements, muscle activity, electrical interference, and other sources of noise.

What does this have to do with machine learning?

This is where things become particularly interesting. Machine-learning models can learn patterns in EEG signals that may be difficult to identify using traditional signal-processing approaches alone.

EEG and inner speech

In my current research, I am interested in whether these signals contain information that can help distinguish different forms of inner or imagined speech.
